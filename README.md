# Awesome-Proxy-Voting-Platform

Top Proxy Voting Platform Ecosystem

Curated List of SaaS Products & Open-Source GitHub Projects
Focused on Institutional Proxy Voting, Shareholder Voting, Proxy Distribution, Vote Processing, Stewardship & Corporate Governance
Last updated: September 2026

This repository tracks notable SaaS/hosted platforms and open-source projects for Proxy Voting and Shareholder Governance. These platforms help institutional investors, asset managers, custodians, brokers, issuers, proxy advisors, pension funds, and governance teams manage shareholder meetings, distribute proxy materials, collect voting instructions, apply voting policies, analyze ballot proposals, manage delegation, and report stewardship activity.

Examples include Broadridge ProxyEdge, Glass Lewis Viewpoint, Institutional Shareholder Services (ISS), ProxyVote, ProxyExchange, Computershare Proxy, Equiniti Proxy, Manifest, Proxy Insight, and VoteTracker (the category leaders).

Open-source emphasis: The open-source landscape is more fragmented than the commercial institutional proxy-voting market because regulated proxy distribution and shareholder-record infrastructure are typically operated by specialized financial intermediaries. However, a strong ecosystem exists for building transparent voting, governance, delegation, ballot-processing, identity, auditability, and shareholder-engagement systems. Open-source building blocks include Snapshot, Vocdoni, OpenZeppelin Governor, Compound Governor, Tally-compatible governance infrastructure, Helios, CIVS, OpaVote-related tools, Decidim, Consul Democracy, Loomio, LiquidFeedback, and numerous cryptographic and verifiable-election projects.

Additional Strong Open-Source Options

Blockchain governance frameworks: Snapshot, Aragon OSx, OpenZeppelin Governor, Compound Governance, DAOstack, Moloch DAO, Tribute DAO, and Colony.

Delegation and liquid democracy: LiquidFeedback, Snapshot delegation strategies, token delegation modules, and custom OpenZeppelin voting implementations.

Cryptographically verifiable voting: Vocdoni, Helios, ElectionGuard, and other end-to-end verifiable election projects.

Ranked-choice and ballot tabulation: CIVS, OpenSTV, PyRankVote, VoteKit, and community election-analysis libraries.

Participatory governance platforms: Decidim, Consul Democracy, Loomio, Adhocracy, DemocracyOS, and Pol.is.

Voting-power engines: Snapshot Strategies, OpenZeppelin Votes modules, ERC20Votes, ERC721Votes, delegated-token models, and custom shareholder-record adapters.

Identity and eligibility layers: Verifiable credentials, decentralized identity frameworks, OpenID/OAuth systems, and institution-managed shareholder registries.

Auditability and transparency: ElectionGuard-style cryptographic verification, blockchain audit trails, signed ballots, append-only logs, and public proposal repositories.

Policy engines: Open Policy Agent-style rules can be combined with voting infrastructure to implement automated voting policies and institutional governance controls.

Many specialized projects exist for DAO governance, delegated voting, quadratic voting, conviction voting, ranked-choice elections, cryptographic ballots, vote tabulation, and verifiable election systems.

Frameworks for building custom systems: Combine Snapshot for flexible off-chain proposal and voting workflows with OpenZeppelin Governor for programmable on-chain governance and auditable execution. Use Vocdoni, Helios, or ElectionGuard where cryptographic verifiability and election integrity are priorities. For institutional-style governance portals, combine an open-source voting engine with a shareholder-record integration layer, LiquidFeedback-style delegation, policy rules, secure identity management, and an immutable audit trail. Decidim, Consul Democracy, and Loomio can provide stakeholder discussion and proposal workflows around the voting process. OpenZeppelin's modular Governor framework is particularly useful when voting power, quorum, counting methods, delegation, and execution rules need to be customized.

Disclaimer

This is a community-curated list — not exhaustive and not an endorsement.

Institutional proxy voting, beneficial ownership, shareholder identification, proxy distribution, and vote tabulation can involve regulated financial-market infrastructure and jurisdiction-specific legal requirements.

Many open-source governance and election projects are not direct drop-in replacements for regulated institutional proxy-voting networks such as broker, custodian, transfer-agent, or central securities depository infrastructure.

Organizations building self-hosted voting systems should carefully address voter eligibility, shareholder record dates, beneficial ownership, delegation, vote reconciliation, privacy, security, auditability, and legal compliance.

Cryptographic or blockchain-based voting can improve transparency and verifiability but does not automatically solve identity verification, coercion resistance, privacy, custody-chain, or regulatory-compliance challenges.

Self-hosted open-source solutions require appropriate security reviews, access controls, key management, backup procedures, audit logging, operational monitoring, and regular dependency updates.
